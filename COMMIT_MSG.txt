Cross-factory validation passes: separation +0.155, zero false duplicates

Six physically distinct factories sampled from the gated repo. The pass
criteria were written into validate.py before that data was reachable --
separation above 0.10, and under 1% of cross-factory pairs clearing the
duplicate threshold. Measured: 0.155 and 0.00%. Not one pair in twenty
thousand.

This settles the standing objection that CLIP might collapse fisheye
industrial footage into an undifferentiated region, which would have made the
redundancy figure an artifact of the encoder rather than a property of the
corpus. It does not extend 27.2% beyond factory 051: validating the method is
not the same as validating the number.

Three bugs fixed on the way, all of them mine:

- np.save appends .npy unless the path already ends in it, so the atomic
  checkpoint wrote <tag>.npy.tmp.npy and the rename found no source. The fix
  I had added to prevent data loss was itself killing runs at the first
  checkpoint. Hand np.save an open file object instead.
- the max-clips cap was checked after the generator had already yielded, so
  every unit pulled and discarded one extra 200 MB clip. Replaced with
  islice, which stops the generator before it fetches.
- added --factory-offset so a run killed mid-corpus resumes where it stopped
  rather than re-downloading what it already has.

Measured rather than assumed, after a first estimate proved wrong: bandwidth
bursts at 10 MB/s but sustains far less, the first clip is 217 MB covering
433 s and 13,003 frames, and keyframe decode costs 0.6 s per 20 frames. The
bottleneck was the duplicate download, not decoding.
